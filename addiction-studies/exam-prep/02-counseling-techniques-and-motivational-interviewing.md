# CADC / CAADE Exam Module 02: Counseling Techniques & Motivational Interviewing

High-yield study material focusing on evidence-based counseling modalities for substance use disorders.

---

## 🔄 1. Transtheoretical Model (Stages of Change)

Developed by Prochaska & DiClemente. Essential for assessment and intervention matching.

```
[Precontemplation] ➔ [Contemplation] ➔ [Preparation] ➔ [Action] ➔ [Maintenance] (🔁 [Relapse])
```

| Stage | Client Mindset | Counselor Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Precontemplation** | No intention to change behavior. Unaware or in denial of problems. | Raise awareness; explore ambivalence gently without confrontation. |
| **Contemplation** | Aware problem exists; considering change but ambivalent ("Yes, but..."). | Tip the balance toward change; explore pros and cons (decisional balance). |
| **Preparation** | Intends to take action soon (within 30 days); taking small steps. | Help client develop an actionable, realistic change plan. |
| **Action** | Actively modifying behavior and environment (0–6 months). | Support skill acquisition, reinforce self-efficacy, prevent relapse. |
| **Maintenance** | Sustaining change long-term (>6 months); consolidating gains. | Identify high-risk situations; reinforce relapse prevention strategies. |
| **Relapse** | Recurrence of substance use. | Reframe as learning opportunity; re-engage client without judgment. |

## 🎯 2. Motivational Interviewing (MI) Principles & OARS

Founded by William R. Miller & Stephen Rollnick. Client-centered, directive method for enhancing intrinsic motivation.

### Core Spirit of MI
- **Partnership / Collaboration**: Working *with* the client, not over them.
- **Acceptance / Autonomy**: Respecting client self-determination.
- **Compassion**: Actively promoting client welfare.
- **Evocation**: Drawing out client's own ideas and motivation ("Change Talk").

### The OARS Skills

- **O — Open-Ended Questions**: Encourage client reflection and dialogue (e.g., *"What are some things you'd like to change about your drinking?"*).
- **A — Affirmations**: Validate client strengths, efforts, and values (e.g., *"It shows great courage that you came in today."*).
- **R — Reflective Listening**: Mirroring client statements to clarify meaning and deepen exploration (e.g., *"You're feeling overwhelmed by stress and alcohol feels like the only relief."*).
- **S — Summaries**: Recapping key points of discussion to transition topics or highlight change talk.

## 💡 3. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Techniques

- **Core Concept**: Thoughts lead to feelings, which drive behaviors. Substance use is a learned coping behavior.
- **Functional Analysis (ABC Model)**:
  - **A — Activating Event / Trigger**: External (party, stress) or Internal (anxiety, craving).
  - **B — Beliefs / Automatic Thoughts**: *"I can't handle this without a drink."*
  - **C — Consequences**: Drinking behavior and subsequent feelings.
- **Cognitive Reframing**: Identifying and challenging cognitive distortions (e.g., all-or-nothing thinking, catastrophizing).
