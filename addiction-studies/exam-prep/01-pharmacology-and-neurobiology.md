## 🧠 1. Neurobiology of Addiction

### The Brain Reward System
- **Primary Pathway**: The **Mesolimbic Dopamine Pathway** (Ventral Tegmental Area [VTA] → Nucleus Accumbens [NAc] → Prefrontal Cortex [PFC]).
- **Dopamine (DA)**: Primary neurotransmitter associated with pleasure, reinforcement, and incentive salience ("wanting").
- **Neuroadaptation**: Chronic substance use alters receptor density (downregulation of DA D2 receptors), leading to anhedonia and increased tolerance.

### Key Neurotransmitters in Addiction
| Neurotransmitter | Primary Functions | Impact of Substances |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dopamine** | Reward, motivation, reinforcement | Stimulated by alcohol, opioids, cocaine, amphetamines. |
| **GABA (Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid)** | Inhibitory (calming), decreases neuronal excitability | Potentiated by Alcohol, Benzodiazepines, Barbiturates. |
| **Glutamate** | Excitatory, memory, learning | Suppressed by alcohol; rebound surge causes withdrawal seizures/delirium. |
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | Mood, sleep, emotional regulation | Altered by MDMA, Hallucinogens, Cocaine. |
| **Endogenous Opioids (Endorphins)** | Analgesia, euphoria, stress reduction | Mimicked by Heroin, Fentanyl, Oxycodone, Morphine. |

## 💊 2. Major Drug Classes & Characteristics

### A. Central Nervous System (CNS) Depressants
- **Substances**: Alcohol, Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Valium), Barbiturates.
- **Mechanism**: Enhances GABA activity, depresses CNS function.
- **Withdrawal Risk**: **Life-threatening** (Delirium Tremens [DTs], seizures, severe autonomic hyperactivity). Requires medical detoxification.

### B. Opioids & Analgesics
- **Substances**: Heroin, Fentanyl, Oxycodone, Hydrocodone, Methadone, Buprenorphine.
- **Mechanism**: Mu-opioid receptor agonists.
- **Signs of Overdose**: Triad of Respiratory Depression, Miosis (pinpoint pupils), Unconsciousness/Coma.
- **Antidote**: **Naloxone (Narcan)** — Competitive opioid antagonist.

### C. CNS Stimulants
- **Substances**: Methamphetamine, Cocaine, Amphetamines, Prescription Stimulants (Adderall, Ritalin).
- **Mechanism**: Blocks reuptake/forces release of Dopamine, Norepinephrine, and Serotonin.
- **Withdrawal**: Intense craving, severe depression, fatigue, psychomotor agitation/retardation ("crashing").

### D. Hallucinogens & Dissociatives
- **Substances**: LSD, Psilocybin, PCP, Ketamine, MDMA.
- **Mechanism**: 5-HT2A agonist (Hallucinogens); NMDA receptor antagonist (Dissociatives like PCP/Ketamine).

## 📊 3. Key Clinical Concepts

- **Tolerance**: Needing increased amounts of a substance to achieve the desired effect or diminished effect with continued use of the same amount.
- **Cross-Tolerance**: Development of tolerance to one drug leading to tolerance of another drug in the same class (e.g., Alcohol and Benzodiazepines).
- **Synergistic Effect**: Combination of two drugs producing an effect greater than the sum of their individual effects (e.g., Alcohol + Benzodiazepines = severe respiratory arrest).
